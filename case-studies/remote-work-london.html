Econometric modelling · labour market analysis

Socioeconomic Dynamics of Remote Work in Greater London

BSc Economics · University of Hertfordshire

1% more overcrowding → 1.59% lower likelihood of WFH
Education a key facilitator of flexible work

Problem

What determines who can work from home in Greater London? The project examined the role of housing conditions and education levels.

Approach

  • Built an econometric model of the likelihood of working from home.
  • Interpreted coefficients on home overcrowding and education.

Findings

  • For every 1% increase in home overcrowding, the likelihood of working from home decreased by 1.59%.
  • Education was identified as a key facilitator of flexible roles.
  • The physical home environment matters as much as individual skills, with implications for HR strategy, urban planning and social policy.

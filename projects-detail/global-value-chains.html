International Production and Governance: Global Value Chains & Developing Economies

OverviewContextConceptsMethodologyDataWhy it mattersSkills gained

Overview

Analyzed the evolution of global production, focusing on the rise of Global Value Chains (GVCs) and "trade in tasks." Evaluated the "smile curve" concept to show how value is distributed across production stages.

Context & question

As production fragments across borders, where is value captured - and what does that mean for developing economies trying to move up?

Key concepts

  • Global value chains (GVCs) and trade in tasks: Production is split into stages performed in different countries, so countries increasingly trade tasks (assembly, design, logistics) rather than finished goods.
  • Smile curve: Plots value added against production stages: high at the ends (R&D, design, branding, after-sales) and low in the middle (manufacturing and assembly). It explains why hosting assembly does not guarantee capturing value.
  • Middle-income trap: Economies that grew on low-cost labour can stall when wages rise but they have not yet built the innovation and skills to compete at the high-value ends of the chain.
  • Foreign direct investment (FDI): Can bring capital, technology and market access, but the gains depend on linkages to local firms and on upgrading.
  • Supply-chain resilience: Shocks like COVID-19 exposed how tightly optimised, geographically concentrated chains transmit disruption, prompting debate on diversification and reshoring.

Methodology & process

  1. Traced how production shifted from national industries to fragmented GVCs.
  2. Linked GVC participation to development outcomes through FDI and the middle-income trap.
  3. Assessed how global shocks such as COVID-19 tested the resilience of these networks.

Why it matters

Provides a deep understanding of how the global economy is organized through fragmented production networks.

Helps identify where value is created in a supply chain, which is essential for global supply chain management and market entry strategy.

Supply-chain, procurement and market-entry decisions hinge on knowing where value and risk sit in a chain. This project builds that map at the level of whole economies.

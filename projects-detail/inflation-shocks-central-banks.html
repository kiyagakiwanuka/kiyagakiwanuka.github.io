Contemporary Issues in Economics: Inflation Shocks and Institutional Responses

OverviewContextConceptsMethodologyWhy it mattersSkills gained

Overview

Researched the impact of recent inflation shocks on the global economic structure and the role of central banks in managing price stability.

Context & question

When inflation spikes, how do central banks restore price stability - and who bears the cost in jobs and incomes?

Key concepts

  • Supply vs demand shocks: Demand-driven inflation responds well to tighter policy; supply shocks (such as energy prices) raise inflation while weakening output, making the policy choice harder.
  • Inflation targeting: Central banks such as the Bank of England set interest rates to bring inflation back to target, relying on credibility to anchor expectations.
  • Transmission mechanism: Rate rises work through borrowing costs, asset prices, the exchange rate and expectations, with long and variable lags.
  • Distributional effects: Inflation and the response to it fall unevenly: lower-income households spend more on essentials, while borrowers, savers and workers in rate-sensitive sectors are affected differently.

Methodology & process

  1. Characterised the recent inflation shocks and their sources.
  2. Examined how central banks responded to protect price stability.
  3. Evaluated the knock-on effects of those interventions on employment and income inequality.
  4. Drew the analysis together in a critical essay.

Why it matters

Connects big-picture economic theories to current events like job availability and earnings.

It demonstrates an ability to analyze complex corporate and labor situations to explain how policy changes impact employees and business models.

Interest-rate decisions feed directly into pricing, wage settlements, credit risk and hiring plans. Understanding the policy reaction function helps an organisation anticipate rather than react.

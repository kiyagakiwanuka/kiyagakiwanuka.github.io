Industry Practice: Economic Policy & The Cost-of-Living Crisis

Overview

Acted as a junior economist to prepare a high-level briefing for the UK Secretary of State.

Context & question

What should government do about soaring energy bills - and should energy producers' extraordinary profits help pay for it?

Key concepts

  • Windfall tax: A one-off or temporary tax on profits judged to arise from external events rather than firm effort or investment. The argument is that taxing such rents distorts behaviour less; the counter-argument is that it can deter future investment and raise policy uncertainty.
  • Targeted vs universal support: Universal measures (such as price caps) are simple but costly and poorly targeted; targeted payments are cheaper per household helped but risk gaps in coverage.
  • Fuel poverty and demographic variation: Energy use and vulnerability differ by age, household type, housing quality and income, so the same price shock hits groups unevenly.

Methodology & process

  1. Took the role of a junior economist writing for the Secretary of State, so prioritised clarity and actionable options.
  2. Evaluated the main policy responses to rising energy costs.
  3. Reviewed energy producers' financial data for 2014–2023 to inform the windfall-tax debate.
  4. Researched how fuel consumption varies across demographic groups to identify who is most exposed.
  5. Condensed the evidence into a high-level briefing with ethical considerations made explicit.

Why it matters

Bridges the gap between raw financial numbers and public policy execution.

Demonstrates an ability to simplify highly technical data into actionable, ethical advice for senior corporate executives or government officials.

Briefing senior decision-makers on a contested issue - with financial evidence, distributional analysis and a clear recommendation on one page - is the core output of policy, public affairs and strategy roles.

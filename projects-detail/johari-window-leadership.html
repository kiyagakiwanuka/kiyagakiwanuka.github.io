Exploring Leadership Potential Through the Johari Window Framework

OverviewContextConceptsMethodologyDataWhy it mattersSkills gained

Overview

Assessed personal leadership traits using the Johari Window model and Goleman’s Emotional Intelligence framework.

Context & question

Where does the way I see myself as a leader differ from how others see me - and what should I do about the gaps?

Key concepts

  • Johari Window: A 2×2 model (Luft and Ingham) with four areas - open, blind, hidden and unknown - formed by what is known to oneself and what is known to others. Feedback shrinks the blind area; self-disclosure shrinks the hidden one.
  • Goleman's emotional intelligence framework: Self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy and social skills as the competencies behind effective leadership.
  • Development planning: Turning feedback into specific, time-bound actions that can be reviewed.

Methodology & process

  1. Gathered self-assessment and peer feedback on leadership traits.
  2. Placed the results in the Johari Window to separate shared strengths from blind spots.
  3. Interpreted the gaps through Goleman's EI competencies.
  4. Designed a structured personal professional development plan from the findings.

Why it matters

Highlights that effective leadership relies heavily on self-awareness, empathy, and transparent communication rather than just technical knowledge.

It proves a commitment to continuous self-improvement and authentic professional relationship building.

Employers look for analysts who seek feedback and act on it. This project demonstrates that habit through a recognised framework.

Neoclassical Economics in the Water Sector

OverviewContextConceptsMethodologyWhy it mattersSkills gained

Overview

Analyzed the application of neoclassical principles (privatization, market-based pricing, and deregulation) to essential water services.

Context & question

What happens when essential water services are run on market principles - and who loses when efficiency and equity collide?

Key concepts

  • Neoclassical policy toolkit: Privatisation, market-based (cost-reflective) pricing and deregulation, justified by the expectation that competition and price signals allocate resources efficiently.
  • Natural monopoly: Water networks have high fixed costs and are uneconomic to duplicate, so privatisation tends to create private monopolies rather than competition, which makes regulation essential.
  • Efficiency vs equity: Prices that reflect cost can improve efficiency but make an essential good less affordable for low-income households, a tension sharper for water than for most goods.
  • Regulatory responses: Price caps, social tariffs and service obligations are the usual tools for protecting vulnerable users.

Methodology & process

  1. Assessed how those principles fit water, an essential good with natural-monopoly features.
  2. Reviewed the literature on the efficiency-equity trade-off.
  3. Investigated the impact of private monopolies on low-income and marginalised communities.

Why it matters

Focuses on the real-world social risks of economic efficiency models.

This skill is critical for regulatory analysis, public-private partnership (PPP) consulting, and corporate ESG reporting.

Regulators, utilities, PPP advisers and ESG teams all have to weigh commercial efficiency against social obligations. This project rehearses that trade-off on one of the most politically sensitive sectors.

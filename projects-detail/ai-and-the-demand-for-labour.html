Assessing the Consequences of Artificial Intelligence on the Demand for Labour

OverviewContextConceptsMethodologyDataWhy it mattersSkills gained

Overview

Evaluated the impact of AI on the labor market by focusing on overall employment levels and changing skill demands.

Context & question

Does AI destroy jobs, or does it reshape which tasks - and which skills - employers pay for?

Key concepts

  • Task-based model (Acemoglu & Restrepo): Treats jobs as bundles of tasks. Technology affects labour demand by changing which tasks machines can perform, rather than replacing whole occupations.
  • Displacement effect: Automation takes over tasks previously done by workers, reducing labour demand for those tasks.
  • Productivity effect: Cheaper production raises output and demand, which can increase labour demand in non-automated tasks and elsewhere in the economy.
  • Reinstatement effect: New tasks are created in which labour has a comparative advantage, pulling workers back in. The net effect on employment depends on the balance of all three.
  • Skill-biased change: Technology can raise demand for some skills while lowering it for others, so aggregate employment can hold steady while the composition of work changes.

Methodology & process

  1. Separated the question into two outcomes: the overall level of employment and the mix of skills demanded.
  2. Used the task-based model to organise the evidence by displacement, productivity and reinstatement effects.
  3. Mapped claims, counter-claims and evidence in a structured argument map before writing.
  4. Wrote a critical essay reaching a reasoned judgement rather than a headline prediction.

Why it matters

Applies advanced economic theory to modern technological shifts.

It moves beyond simple job-loss narratives to understand how technology reshapes labor demand at a granular level, which is valuable for strategic workforce consulting.

Workforce planning, HR strategy and economic consulting all need a disciplined way to discuss technology and jobs. The framework gives a vocabulary for that conversation that goes beyond 'robots take jobs'.

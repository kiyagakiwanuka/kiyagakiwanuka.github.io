Macroeconomic Performance: Indicators, Objectives, and Global Trends

OverviewContextConceptsMethodologyDataWhy it mattersSkills gained

Overview

Analyzed key performance indicators (KPIs) including real GDP growth, unemployment rates, and inflation.

Context & question

How do you judge whether an economy is performing well - and what has to be traded off to improve it?

Key concepts

  • Core indicators: Real GDP growth (output adjusted for inflation), the unemployment rate, inflation (e.g. CPI) and living-standard measures such as GDP per capita.
  • Macroeconomic objectives: Policy typically pursues stable growth, low unemployment, price stability and a sustainable external balance; these goals can pull against each other.
  • Phillips curve: Describes a short-run inverse relationship between unemployment and inflation. Expectations-augmented versions (Friedman, Phelps) imply no permanent trade-off, and the relationship has shifted and flattened over time.
  • Comparative analysis: Comparing economies on the same indicators separates global shocks from country-specific policy choices.

Methodology & process

  1. Defined the indicator set and what each reveals about economic health.
  2. Evaluated the four pillars of macroeconomic policy and the tensions between them.
  3. Compared performance across different economies on common measures.
  4. Used the Phillips curve to analyse the trade-off between inflation and unemployment.

Why it matters

Provides a robust framework for diagnosing the health of an economy.

It builds the ability to translate raw numbers (like CPI and GDP per capita) into clear economic narratives to identify sectoral sensitivities and market risks.

Economic, credit and market-risk teams translate indicator releases into a view on conditions. This project practises that translation, from numbers to narrative to risk.

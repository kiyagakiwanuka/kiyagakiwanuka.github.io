Economic Justifications for State Investment Banks (SIBs)

OverviewContextConceptsMethodologyWhy it mattersSkills gained

Overview

Analyzed public banks as tools to correct market failures, handle externalities, and provide public goods.

Context & question

Is there an economic case for state investment banks - and when do they add something private finance cannot?

Key concepts

  • Market failure: Private markets under-supply some investment, for example because of information problems, long horizons or risks that private lenders will not hold.
  • Externalities and public goods: Investments with wide social spill-overs (infrastructure, basic research, green energy) earn private returns below their social value, so they are under-financed.
  • Countercyclical lending: Private credit contracts in downturns; public banks can maintain or expand lending to stabilise investment through the cycle.
  • The entrepreneurial state (Mazzucato): Argues that the state has often been the early, risk-taking investor behind major technologies, shaping and creating markets rather than merely fixing their failures.

Methodology & process

  1. Set out the market-failure rationale for public banks.
  2. Assessed their role in financing externalities and public goods.
  3. Evaluated their countercyclical lending role during recessions.
  4. Contrasted market-fixing and market-shaping (entrepreneurial-state) views of public investment.

Why it matters

Explores how different economic theories shape long-term industrial growth strategies.

It provides valuable insights for roles in public finance or strategic consulting on how public and private sectors can co-create markets.

Public finance, infrastructure, development banking and strategy roles increasingly involve co-investment between public and private sectors. Knowing the economic logic for each side's role makes those partnerships easier to structure and justify.

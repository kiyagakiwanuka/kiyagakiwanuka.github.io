Overview

Conducted a risk-return analysis of five major company shares (Netflix, Amazon, Google, NVIDIA, and RIO) from 2014 to 2018.

Context & question

How should capital be allocated across five major company shares to get the best risk-adjusted return?

Key concepts

  • Modern Portfolio Theory (MPT): Markowitz's insight that portfolio risk depends not only on each asset's volatility but on how assets co-move; combining imperfectly correlated assets can lower risk without sacrificing expected return.
  • Variance-covariance matrix: Holds every asset's variance and every pairwise covariance; with matrix algebra, portfolio variance is w'Σw, where w is the vector of weights.
  • Efficient frontier: The set of portfolios offering the highest expected return for each level of risk. Portfolios below it are dominated: another mix delivers more return for the same risk.
  • Sharpe ratio: Excess return over the risk-free rate divided by standard deviation - return per unit of risk. The maximum-Sharpe portfolio is the tangency point between the frontier and the capital market line.
  • Linear and non-linear programming with Solver: Excel Solver searches for weights that maximise an objective (the Sharpe ratio) subject to constraints such as weights summing to one.

Methodology & process

  1. Used matrix algebra to calculate the return and risk of an equally weighted portfolio.
  2. Constructed an efficient frontier from 10 different allocation weights.
  3. Used Excel Solver (linear programming) to find the allocation that maximises the Sharpe Ratio.

Findings & outcomes

  • Visualised the risk/return trade-off across allocations with the efficient frontier.
  • Identified the Sharpe-maximising allocation as the optimal risk-adjusted portfolio.

Why it matters

Applies complex mathematical tools (matrix algebra and linear programming) to solve real-world financial problems.

Building an efficient frontier demonstrates how to visualize risk and optimize capital allocation for high risk-adjusted returns.

Risk-adjusted thinking underpins asset management, treasury and corporate finance. Building the frontier from first principles - rather than reading it off a tool - shows the mechanics behind every allocation recommendation.

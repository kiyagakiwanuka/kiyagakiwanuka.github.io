Overview

Produced a comprehensive 4,000-word corporate financial report evaluating the economic health and structural stability of Everton FC over a five-year period (2020–2025).

Context & question

How financially healthy and structurally stable is Everton FC, given ownership changes, stadium construction and Premier League Profit & Sustainability Rules (PSR)?

Key concepts

  • Return on capital employed (ROCE): Operating profit divided by capital employed: how efficiently a business turns its long-term funding into profit, central when a club invests heavily in a stadium.
  • Current ratio: Current assets divided by current liabilities: a measure of short-term liquidity and the ability to meet near-term obligations.
  • Gearing: Debt relative to equity or capital: the degree to which a business relies on borrowing, and therefore its exposure to interest costs and refinancing risk.
  • Wage-to-turnover ratio: A key football-finance metric: high ratios leave little margin for other costs and signal pressure under spending rules.
  • Price inelasticity: When demand barely responds to price, as for loyal matchday supporters, revenue can rise with price, though at a reputational cost.
  • Profit and Sustainability Rules (PSR): Premier League rules limiting losses over a rolling period, linking on-pitch spending to financial discipline.

Methodology & process

  1. Analysed five years of financial performance (2020–2025).
  2. Calculated and benchmarked ratios including ROCE, Current Ratio and Gearing.
  3. Evaluated ownership transitions, matchday ticket price inelasticity and wage-to-turnover trajectories.
  4. Assessed capital efficiency relating to the new stadium construction.

Findings & outcomes

  • Exposed core operating losses in the club's business.
  • Set out how competitive spending must be balanced with financial discipline under PSR, and how stadium commercialisation and private equity takeovers affect sustainability.

Why it matters

Addresses how sports organizations must balance competitive spending with strict financial discipline (such as Premier League PSR rules) to ensure corporate sustainability.

It serves as a blueprint for navigating stadium infrastructure commercialization and private equity takeovers.

The same toolkit - ratio analysis, benchmarking and reading ownership and capital-project risk - transfers directly to credit analysis, equity research and corporate finance in any sector.

Overview

Worked as sole consultant to a Super League Basketball club on a live commercial question: could it sustainably run a weekly recreational session for adults over 40, and should it fund that session through a Sport England grant or through participant fees.

Key concepts

  • Contribution margin and break-even: Contribution is price minus variable cost per participant; break-even volume is fixed costs divided by contribution. Testing several price points shows how sensitive viability is to pricing.
  • Market sizing: Estimating the addressable population from official data (here ONS Census 2021) before forecasting uptake.
  • Weighted multi-criteria decision analysis (MCDA): Scores options against several criteria, weighted by importance, so that a decision with competing objectives becomes transparent.
  • Sensitivity analysis: Re-running the ranking under many randomised weightings tests whether the recommendation depends on a particular set of assumptions.
  • Sampling bias: When a survey reaches a different population from the target one, conclusions about the target are unreliable. Recognising this is as important as the data itself.

Methodology & process

  1. Designed and distributed a primary survey (97 eligible responses) and interviewed the founder of the only comparable provider in Sheffield.
  2. Benchmarked court hire, sessional coaching and affiliation costs across the city.
  3. Traced the club's corporate structure through Companies House filings to establish which entity could apply for public money.
  4. Built a contribution-margin model testing break-even across six price points and two cost scenarios.
  5. Ranked the two funding pathways with weighted multi-criteria decision analysis, stress-tested against 20,000 randomised reweightings of the criteria.

Findings & outcomes

  • The survey reached lapsed and current players, not the inactive adults Sport England funds: 85.6% had played basketball before and only 6.2% recorded a week without a single 30-minute bout of activity.
  • This contradicted the premise of the brief. The sampling limitation became the basis of the recommendation: launch commercially for the audience the evidence found, and defer the grant application to year two once a running pilot can evidence the reach a funder scores against.
  • Delivered a 6,000-word report with a costed launch plan, a risk register and a staged measurement framework.

Why it matters

That contradicted the premise the brief started from.

Demonstrates the ability to test a client’s preferred route against primary data, tell them it fails, and hand back a costed alternative they can act on.
